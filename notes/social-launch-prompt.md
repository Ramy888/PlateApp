## The app

- **Name:** The Plate
- **Line:** One small thing, added to what you already eat.
- **Link (use this exact URL, in every post that needs one):**
  https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.platepatch.app
- **Platform:** Android, live on Google Play now (v1.4.7)

**What it does.** You tell it whether it's breakfast, lunch, dinner or a snack,
and tap what's on the plate. It works out what the meal is light on — protein,
fibre, or healthy fats — and gives three ways to fix it: the fastest, the
cheapest, and a plant-based one. Afterwards it asks one question: still hungry,
comfortably satisfied, or too full? That one answer is the only thing it learns
from.

**What it deliberately is not.** Not a calorie tracker. No counting, no
weighing, no logging, no streaks, no guilt. That is the whole point, not a
missing feature.

**How it's built** (for the technical posts): Flutter, with the suggestion
engine running entirely on-device so the core works offline and free. The AI
features — photograph a meal, chat, voice, and a drawn picture of your patched
plate — run on Cloudflare Workers with Gemini and Workers AI, and need a sign-in.
RevenueCat handles subscriptions. Google Play Integrity guards the API.

**Something genuinely distinctive:** the food catalogue covers Egyptian and MENA
dishes — fuul, taameya, koshari, molokhia, mahshi, shawarma — which most
nutrition apps simply don't have.

- **Never post a promo code.** Public codes get scraped within hours and each

- Don't claim download numbers, revenue, ratings, or user counts. I have none
  to quote and invented ones are worse than silence.
- Don't say it gives medical or dietary advice. It suggests food.
- No em dashes. Plain punctuation.
- No hashtag spam: at most 2-3 per post, none on LinkedIn.
- Every post must be able to stand alone. Assume the reader has never heard of
  the app.
