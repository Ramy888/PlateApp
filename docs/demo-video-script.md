# Demo video — shot list and script

Under 2:00. Public on YouTube or Vimeo. Real device. No copyrighted music.

**Record on 1.4.6, not the live 1.4.5.** 1.4.5 still draws the wrong picture for
a food it does not recognise and shows no suggestions for it — both on camera in
the first thirty seconds if you photograph the wrong breakfast. Install 1.4.6
from the internal track, or sideload it, before you shoot.

**Setup:** Do Not Disturb on. Already signed in, Pro active. Show taps on.
Screen recorder at 1080p, sound set to **Media sounds**. Phone in a stand for
the scan shot.

Record clean, add the narration afterwards on the Mac. Trying to narrate live
while holding a phone over a plate is how takes get wasted.

---

## The sequencing decision

**Open with the scan, not the voice.** Both work, and scan wins for a reason
worth stating: it is visual from the first frame and its payoff — your own plate
redrawn with the addition on it — lands in about twenty seconds. Voice is the
more distinctive feature but it opens with a person holding a button in silence,
which is a poor first ten seconds of a judged video.

Voice goes second, where it reads as "and it also does this".

---

## 0:00 – 0:12 · The premise

**Screen:** home. "What are you eating?" with the four ways in.

**Narration:**
> Most nutrition apps start by telling you the meal in front of you is wrong.
> Count it, weigh it, log it. Almost nobody keeps that up.
> The Plate asks one question instead: what is one thing you could add to this,
> right now?

Hold on the home screen. Do not tap yet. Let the sentence land.

---

## 0:12 – 0:40 · Photograph a meal

**Taps:** camera button → frame a real plate → shutter.

**Narration:**
> Photograph it.

Then silence while it reads. The spinner is about three seconds — leave it. A
judge needs to see that this is a real model call and not a canned demo.

**Screen:** the scan result page. Chips appear: what it found.

**Narration:**
> It reads the plate, and shows you what it found — as things you can correct.
> Remove anything it got wrong.

**Taps:** remove one chip. **Pause on the suggestions changing.**

> The suggestion updates as you correct it. That is not a model re-running —
> the engine is a deterministic rule, so the same plate always gives the same
> answer. The AI reads the photograph; it does not decide what you should eat.

That last line is the one to get right. It is the architectural claim, it is in
the written submission, and this is where a judge can watch it be true.

---

## 0:40 – 1:00 · The patched plate

**Screen:** already on it. Tabs at the top: *With the addition* / *Your photo*.

**Narration:**
> And here is the plate with that one thing on it.

**Taps:** tap **Your photo**, then back to **With the addition**.

> Your own meal, and the version with the addition. No calories, no grams,
> no weighing. That rule is enforced by a test, not a promise.

**Taps:** tap a different suggestion card. The picture redraws.

> Three ways to fix the same plate — fastest, cheapest, plant-based.

---

## 1:00 – 1:22 · Say it instead

**Taps:** back → microphone.

**Narration:**
> Or just say it.

**Action:** hold the button, say clearly:
> "I'm having koshari and a glass of milk."

Let go. **Leave the audio in** — it answers out loud, and that is the point of
the feature. Do not talk over it.

**Taps:** tap **Hear what you said**.

> It plays back exactly what it heard, so when an answer looks wrong you can
> tell a mishearing from a misunderstanding.

Koshari is deliberate: it is in the catalogue, it is Egyptian, and it
demonstrates the MENA claim without needing to say it.

---

## 1:22 – 1:42 · What it costs

**Taps:** settings → subscription, or the Get Pro button.

**Narration:**
> Building a meal by hand is free, offline, signed out, forever.
> The AI features are three free meals, then Plate Pro.

**Screen:** the paywall. **Both prices visible without scrolling** — they are
pinned above the button now, which is worth a beat on screen.

> Subscriptions run through RevenueCat. The app never decides whether you have
> paid — the server checks with RevenueCat and answers accordingly.

---

## 1:42 – 2:00 · Close

**Screen:** back to a patched plate, or the home screen.

**Narration:**
> One small thing, added to what you already eat.
> The Plate. On Google Play now.

Hold the final frame two seconds. Do not fade to black with a logo animation —
it eats time and adds nothing.

## What to leave out, and why

- **Sign-in.** Twenty seconds of Google's account sheet, none of it yours.
  Be signed in before you record.
- **Onboarding.** It is good, and it is not what is being judged.
- **The chat screen.** It overlaps with voice and costs fifteen seconds to make
  the same point.
- **Saved plates and history.** Real features, no payoff on camera.
- **Anything that failed on the day.** If the scan misreads, shoot it again.
  A judge cannot tell a bad take from a bad product.

## If a take goes wrong

The riskiest shot is the scan, because it depends on a live model call and your
lighting. Shoot that section three or four times before moving on, and keep the
best. Everything else is deterministic and will behave the same every time.

## Checks before uploading

- Under 2:00. Devpost says judges are not required to watch past two minutes.
- No music unless you are certain of the licence. The app's own voice is better
  content than a stock track.
- No other company's branding on screen — watch for the Play Store if you show
  a promo code being redeemed. Simplest fix: do not show that flow.
- Public or unlisted on YouTube. Both count as publicly visible.
- Watch it once at full length before you paste the link. Every judge is
  required to watch two minutes of it; you should have watched it too.
